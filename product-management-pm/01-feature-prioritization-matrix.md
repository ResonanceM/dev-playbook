# Feature Prioritization: RICE, MoSCoW, and Opportunity Cost

Prioritization is choosing what not to do. Frameworks create consistency, but scores are estimates—not objective truth.

## Start with strategy

Check alignment with target users, product outcomes, strategic themes, constraints, and current bottlenecks. A high-scoring feature that does not support strategy may still be the wrong investment.

## RICE

A common model:

`RICE = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort`

Define the time horizon and units consistently. Reach should be a count of affected users/accounts in that horizon. Impact should use a shared scale. Confidence should reflect evidence quality. Effort should include design, engineering, QA, rollout, and ongoing operations.

Do not compare scores built from incompatible assumptions.

## MoSCoW

- **Must:** essential for the agreed release outcome.
- **Should:** important, but a viable release exists without it.
- **Could:** useful if capacity remains.
- **Won't (this time):** explicitly deferred.

MoSCoW is useful for scope negotiation, not a substitute for strategic prioritization.

## Other useful lenses

- Cost of delay and time criticality.
- Opportunity solution trees and evidence quality.
- User pain severity and frequency.
- Risk reduction and enablement.
- Regulatory or contractual necessity.
- Maintenance and operational cost.

## Prioritization workshop

1. Agree the outcome and constraints.
2. Define candidate opportunities and evidence.
3. Estimate reach, impact, confidence, and effort.
4. Identify dependencies and risk.
5. Compare against the opportunity cost of alternatives.
6. Decide, document, and set a review date.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a numerical score as a decision.
- Counting feature requests instead of understanding problems.
- Ignoring maintenance and support cost.
- Prioritizing only loud stakeholders.
- Committing to scope before discovery.
